2 / 4 · What's wrong

The three most serious findings

  1. The QC reference is in the data, the alignment, the tree and the figure. All 610 of 610 bp are identical to PX696973.1 (Lyon lab culture). It is the nearest relative of crsp (99.2 %) and easy (98.3 %).
  2. The tree figure shows support values on the wrong branches. Re-rooting shifted labels on 20 of 233 nodes, and 10 “strong” dots are wrong. The report's “81–95 %” for the genome-derived samples is really 74 % and 59 %.
  3. The download counts 139 GenBank records a second time as “BOLD-only”. 132 of the 2,088 “not in GenBank” records have the same sequence as a GenBank record. crsp's “4 equally close” hits are really 2 sequences counted twice.
3 / 4 · What checks out

Recomputed and confirmed

  • All 40 nearest-relative identities reproduced exactly by a full, uncapped search.
  • BLAST agrees on the lineage for 38 of 40 samples.
  • sp. O clade: 18 samples with Argentine, Chilean and Peruvian records, UFBoot 99.
  • Nevada 100, Alberta 99, France 100; Titicaca trio placement weak (55).
  • Identity ranges are all right: every sample ≥ 97.2 %, 36 of 40 ≥ 98 %.
  • No sequence the QC dropped is closer to any sample than its reported relative.
  • QC counts: 8 short, 58 low-identity, 40 of 40 ours passed, 1,949 unique.
  • Pipeline numbers: 236 tips, 1,570 → 638 columns, TIM+F+I+R5, constraint applied.
4 / 4 · What needs a human

Only someone who knows these samples

  • Does this lab handle the Lyon H. azteca culture or its DNA?
  • Were the genome animals collected at the same Atacama sites as the PCR animals?
  • Is H. sp. C2 “3TK10” really Titicaca, or the same population as sp. O?

  • Why do 56 Canadian BOLD H. azteca records fail the cox1 filter?
